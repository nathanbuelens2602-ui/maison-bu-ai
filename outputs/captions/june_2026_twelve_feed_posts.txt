─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  POST 01 — Monday, Week 1
  Pillar: The Maison
  Format: Single image (editorial still)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

VISUAL DIRECTION
The atelier in June morning light. Wide angle — architecture, not people.
The concrete floor, the tall windows, the dark metal structures.
Light entering from the left. Soft. Warm. Golden at the edges.
The space is empty. The subject is the space itself.

CAPTION
Something changes in June.

It begins with the light. The way it moves through the windows
at a different angle than it did in May. Slower. More golden.
The stone holds it differently. The mirrors return it, softer.

We notice these things at Maison BU. They are not irrelevant to
what we do. The atmosphere of the atelier shapes the experience
of every guest who steps inside — and June shapes the atmosphere
more than any other month.

Welcome to The Sweetness of June.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  POST 03 — Friday, Week 1
  Pillar: The Maison (Founder voice)
  Format: Single image — Kristof portrait
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

VISUAL DIRECTION
Kristof — natural, unhurried. Not posed for the camera.
Perhaps reading, or at the window, or in consultation.
The quality of his presence is the subject. Not his face —
his attention. The concentration of someone who cares.
Warm light. The atelier behind him.

CAPTION
In summer, the work becomes about ease.

Not simplicity — ease. There is a difference.
Simplicity removes. Ease holds the complexity
of the person while making it invisible to the eye.

It requires more from us, not less.
Understanding the weight of someone's hair.
The way it lifts in humidity, how it holds in heat.
The shape that serves both the morning after and the evening before.

That is the summer craft. Learning, again, to resist
the impulse to do more. — Kristof

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  POST 04 — Monday, Week 2
  Pillar: The Ritual
  Format: Single image — beachwaves macro
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

VISUAL DIRECTION
Extreme close-up of a finished beachwave section in warm light.
Not a selfie angle. Not a mirror shot. The hair itself.
The texture, the weight, the three different qualities of light
caught within a single wave. Golden, alive, individual.
Nothing over-styled. The hair looks like it chose to be this way.

CAPTION
The wand lifts. The curl releases.
Not into place — but into itself.

Beachwaves at Maison BU are never a formula.
They are a conversation: about the weight of your hair,
the shape of your face, how you want to move through summer.
What effortless means — to you, specifically.

The result should look like nothing happened.
Only that something settled.

Two images — same person, same warm light, same window.

Image 2: After — the finished beachwaves. Still real. Still her.

She came in carrying a Tuesday.

wearing it. Hers does now.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  POST 06 — Saturday, Week 2
  Pillar: The Edit
  Format: Single image — colour work detail
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

VISUAL DIRECTION
A close-up of finished colour — dimensional, warm, alive in the light.
Not a selfie. The hair as object of beauty.
The light source: a window. The hair catches three or four different
tones depending on the angle. This is the art.

CAPTION
Colour, at Maison BU, is never about change.

It is about the light that was always inside the hair —
revealed more clearly. Made more present.
The dimension that catches an afternoon differently
than a morning. The warmth that deepens in the sun
and softens in the shade.

Trend-led colour belongs somewhere else.
Here, the only guide is the person wearing it —
their skin, their history, the version of themselves
they are moving toward.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  POST 07 — Monday, Week 3
  Pillar: The Transformation (Ceremony)
  Format: Single editorial image — bridal/event hair
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

VISUAL DIRECTION
A finished ceremonial style — not a bridal cliché.
Warm, refined, deeply personal. No tiara, no veil in shot.
Just: the architecture of the hair, the quality of the light,
the feeling of someone who is completely ready.
Shot from behind or from the side — never a posed frontal portrait.

CAPTION
Some days ask more of us.

The morning of a wedding is one of them. Not just for the bride —
but for everyone in the room where the work happens.
Every pin placed with the understanding that this is not a haircut.
It is an act of care for someone in a significant moment.

At Maison BU, ceremony has always been part of the craft.
It is not a separate service. It is simply the ordinary care —
applied to an extraordinary day.

Every June, Belgium holds its breath for a day.

VISUAL DIRECTION
A guest stepping out of the atelier into the Hasselt summer street.
Seen from inside — she is in the light, we are in the warm shadow.
Her hair moves as she steps out. She does not look back.
She does not need to. She knows.

CAPTION
She arrived carrying the weight of a week.

She left carrying only herself.

That is what we work toward at Maison BU. Not the look —
the feeling. The quiet shift that happens somewhere between
the consultation and the door.

She paused on the step, briefly, and let the June light
touch her hair before she moved into the rest of her day.
We stayed inside and watched her go.

It is a good month to step back into the world feeling ready.
