Every output reviewed below against this standard.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  01 — MONTHLY CONTENT STRATEGY
  "La Douceur de Juin"
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VERDICT: ✓ Passes

WHY IT FEELS LIKE MAISON BU:

The strategy is built around an emotional arc, not a posting schedule.
Each week has a feeling ("The Light Has Changed," "The Ritual of Movement")
before it has a content brief. This mirrors how Maison BU itself thinks —
through emotion, not through content buckets.

The five pillars are weighted correctly: The Ritual and The Transformation
lead, L'Invitation closes. The guest is never sold to before they are
first moved. The booking content only arrives in Week 4 — after three weeks
of world-building have created genuine desire.

The seasonal intelligence is applied correctly: June is treated as
"the arrival of softness" rather than "start of summer sale season."
Belgian cultural context (wedding season, end-of-year events, the first
long evenings) is woven in without being forced.

The hashtag bank prioritises niche, owned and craft-specific tags over
mass-reach beauty tags. This is correct — Maison BU attracts the right
audience, not the largest one.

WHAT TO WATCH:
Ensure the team understands that Week 1 is entirely world-building.
No booking pressure at all. The content only works if the first three
weeks build genuine emotional desire. Resist the temptation to accelerate.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  02 — FOUR INSTAGRAM CAPTIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VERDICT: ✓ Passes across all four

CAPTION 01 (The Ritual — Beachwaves)
  Opening: "The wand lifts. The curl releases. / It falls — not into place,
  but into itself." — This is unmistakably Maison BU. "Not into place,
  but into itself" is a distinction only a brand that understands identity
  would make. The technical (the wand) gives way immediately to the
  philosophical (what the result means). No salon clichés. No exclamation
  marks. The beachwave is positioned as a personal revelation,
  not a styling technique.

  Language check: ✓ No forbidden words. "Reserve your summer ritual"
  (not "book now"). Hashtags are curated and specific.

CAPTION 02 (The Transformation — Signature Cut)
  "She arrived with something she couldn't quite name — a feeling that
  her hair had been belonging to someone else for a while."
  This is the brand at its best. It addresses the exact pain point of
  the guest (feeling misaligned, belonging to someone else) without
  ever naming it bluntly. The caption ends with her at the door,
  "looking at the light" — not at her reflection. She has crossed a
  threshold. That is a Maison BU moment.

  Language check: ✓ No "transformation," no "makeover," no "glow up."
  The word "listening" used correctly and powerfully.

CAPTION 03 (The Maison — Kristof's voice)
  Written in first person as Kristof — "June light does something
  to this atelier." This is the correct use of the founder's voice:
  personal, philosophical, never promotional. The insight about
  "effortlessness requiring mastery" is the kind of idea that builds
  genuine trust over time. It teaches without being didactic.

  Language check: ✓ "Ease requires mastery. It requires knowing exactly
  what to remove, what to leave, where to let the hair decide for itself."
  This is Maison BU brand philosophy, articulated correctly.

CAPTION 04 (L'Invitation — June bookings)
  Opens with an atmospheric observation: "The light in the atelier stays
  warm until nearly nine now." By the time the booking mention arrives,
  the guest has been transported. "The atelier is ready. The calendar is
  nearly full. A window remains for those who know." — The phrase "for
  those who know" creates quiet exclusivity without arrogance. The CTA
  ("We would be glad to receive you") is human, warm, never pressured.

  Language check: ✓ No fake urgency. No discount language. "Receive"
  instead of "welcome" or "see." Link offered, never pushed.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  03 — FOUR STORY FLOWS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VERDICT: ✓ Passes

STORY FLOW 01 (The Art of Beachwaves):
  Correctly structured — desire first (the craft close-up), context
  second (the philosophy), the booking last. The three-slide arc creates
  a micro-emotional journey. The viewer is moved before they are invited.
  "Not every wave is made the same" is a strong opening line for Stories —
  it makes the viewer stop and wonder why.

STORY FLOW 02 (What to Book Before Summer):
  This is the most functionally useful flow — it guides the guest without
  feeling like a menu or a promotion. The key is that each service is
  described through its emotional purpose, not its technical features.
  "The Signature Cut — for the version of you that summer calls forward"
  is selling nothing. It is simply naming what the guest already wants.

STORY FLOW 03 (A Morning in the Atelier):
  The most brand-world story — it reveals the humanity behind the atelier.
  The timestamp "8:47. Before the first guest arrives" is a masterstroke
  of intimacy. It makes the world feel real and unperformed.
  Crucially, the final slide ("Every guest deserves someone who was ready
  for them") has no CTA, no link, no ask. It only offers. This is the
  correct application of the brand's hospitality philosophy in content.

STORY FLOW 04 (A Few Moments Remain):
  Tests the limits of booking content — but passes. The opening slide
  ("June is nearly full") is stated as fact, not manufactured urgency.
  The visual (the empty chair in afternoon light) does the emotional
  work. By the time the booking link appears, the desire is genuine.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  04 — TWO CINEMATIC REEL CONCEPTS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VERDICT: ✓ Passes — strong

REEL 01 (The Beachwaves Ritual — 45s):
  Shot list is production-ready and philosophically correct. The macro
  lens direction, the 120fps slow-motion release, the absence of faces
  until the final beat — all align with the Maison BU visual language
  described in training (Loewe, Aesop, Jacquemus references).
  The music direction (Sébastien Tellier, Air, Nils Frahm) creates a
  specifically European summer sensibility — not generic "luxury" spa
  music. The on-screen text appears only at 00:38 — the viewer has been
  in the world of Maison BU for 38 full seconds before any brand claim
  is made. This is the correct ratio of atmosphere to information.

REEL 02 (The Lightest Version — 30s, Signature Cut):
  The most sophisticated script of the campaign. The structure
  (outside world → door → interior → consultation → cut → departure)
  mirrors the guest journey map exactly: arrival, slowdown, attention,
  transformation, leaving. The reveal is deliberately not a reveal —
  we see the guest's face, not the mirror. This reframes what a
  "transformation" means at Maison BU: internal, personal, private.
  The exterior shots being "slightly cooler" before the interior warmth
  takes over is a colour grade choice that reinforces the brand idea
  without saying a word about it. The sound design (the door closing,
  the silence of the atelier) communicates the brand promise —
  "the outside world softens immediately" — without explanation.

BOTH REELS: No voiceover, no price mention, no service menu, no logo
slam. Correct. The brand is present in the atmosphere, not the overlay.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  05 — MAILCHIMP EMAIL CAMPAIGN
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VERDICT: ✓ Passes — strong

Subject line "The light in the atelier has changed" passes the test:
  — It is personal (the atelier is a specific, known world to the reader)
  — It creates sensory curiosity without clickbait
  — It does not mention a service, a price or a promotion
  — It sounds like it was written by a person

The email body does not mention a service until the second body paragraph.
The opening is entirely atmospheric — June light, the stone, the mirrors.
By the time "beachwaves" and "Signature Cut" are named, the guest is
already inside the world of the atelier and the services feel inevitable.

"The craft is ours. The ease is yours." is the best line in the email.
It completes the effortlessness philosophy in two sentences and
gives the guest a role in the story. It is not a sales line.
It is a gift.

CTA button copy: "Reserve your summer ritual" — not "Book Now."
Not "Shop Services." The verb "reserve" and the noun "ritual" do all
the positioning work without a single extra word.

HTML design: warm off-white background (#F4F1EC), dark top and bottom bars,
serif typeface, generous white space, single column. No images required.
The copy is the design. This is correct for Maison BU.

Sign-off: "With warmth from the atelier — Kristof & the Maison BU team"
Not "Best regards." Not "The Team." A person. A place. Warmth.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  06 — FIVE BOOKING CTAs
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VERDICT: ✓ Passes across all five

The five CTAs form a complete system across all channels:
  Instagram Feed / Stories / Email / SMS / Story standalone
Each is calibrated differently for its context and medium — but all
share the same philosophical foundation: invitation over persuasion.

The most powerful: CTA 04 (SMS — "We thought of you").
This three-word phrase is the most potent tool in Maison BU's
last-minute booking language. It implies selection, not broadcast.
It makes a mass message feel personal. Used sparingly, it will
convert the exact right guests — the ones who value feeling chosen.

The most sophisticated: CTA 05 (Story — "The light in the atelier
is particularly beautiful this time of year"). This line does not
sell a service. It sells a reason to come — atmosphere, beauty,
the specific quality of June light. The booking is almost a
side note. The guest books because they want to experience the thing
described, not because they were pressured.

Universal principles section at the bottom of the CTA file serves
as a brand guardian — a checklist for anyone writing booking content
in the future who may not have internalised the full brand philosophy.
